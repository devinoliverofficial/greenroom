Greenroom linked cards: privacy

Last updated: October 11, 2026

Greenroom is a budget app for touring bands. A tour manager can connect the bank or card company their tour cards are with, through Plaid, so the charges on those cards file themselves onto their tour. This page explains what that connection reads, what Greenroom keeps, and what it never touches.

What Greenroom can do

  • Read charges. That’s all. Greenroom asks Plaid for one thing only: transactions.
  • It cannot move money. Greenroom never asks for payments, transfers or anything else that moves money, and its server refuses every Plaid request except reading accounts and transactions and connecting or disconnecting a bank.

What Greenroom reads through Plaid

  • The names and types of your card, checking and savings accounts (for example “Business Gold Card”), so you can choose which ones feed your tour.
  • Transactions on those accounts: date, amount and merchant name. For an account you switch off, Greenroom keeps nothing about its lines. At most it counts how many there were, so it can tell you they were not looked at.
  • For each bank account you mark for income (merch, guarantees or both): the date and amount of its deposits, to confirm that what atVenu said a show would pay, or a show’s guarantee, actually arrived.
  • For each credit card you log: its balance when you start logging a tour, put on that tour under Credit card, and the payments you make on it afterwards. At each refresh Greenroom also reads the card’s current balance and shows it as Owed on cards. Only that one latest number is kept (each new reading replaces the last), and it never changes what the tour spent.

What Greenroom never reads or keeps

  • Your bank password. You sign in on Plaid’s screen or your bank’s own site, never inside Greenroom.
  • Full card numbers, account numbers or routing numbers. Greenroom keeps only an account’s last four digits, as part of its name, so you can tell your cards apart.
  • Bank account balances. Plaid includes them when it lists your accounts; Greenroom throws them away. The only balances kept are a credit card’s, as described above.
  • Money coming into your bank accounts. Of that, Greenroom keeps at most a count of how many lines there were, with two exceptions. Deposits on accounts you mark for income, and from those only each deposit’s date and amount. And, from the October 2026 update to the bank feed on, money coming back from something you sent (a returned Zelle, wire or payment), which is kept and shown in your pile the way a refund is. From the same update on, a line Greenroom cannot read properly is kept with whatever could be read, so that it is not lost, but never when it is money coming into a bank account.

What Greenroom keeps

  • The key Plaid issues for each bank you connect. It is locked (encrypted) on Greenroom’s server, where only the server can open it. It is never sent to your phone or anyone else’s.
  • Spending it looks at: the date, amount, a cleaned-up merchant name, the account’s name and a category. Charges Greenroom isn’t sure about wait in your pile until they are added to a tour or set aside (who can see the pile is listed further down). Refunds on a card wait in the same pile. For money sent, the bank’s own description is kept in place of a merchant name, with long numbers taken out, because it is the only thing that says who was paid. That covers a Zelle, a wire and a transfer and, from the October 2026 update to the bank feed on, a cash withdrawal, a loan payment, money sent or advanced from a card, and money coming back from any of those. The description can carry a name, a phone number or an email address: the one the money went to, and sometimes your own.
  • Charges on your tours: once added, a charge is part of that tour’s budget.
  • Lines it leaves out of your pile: paying a credit card (and that payment arriving on the card), money moved to your own savings or investments, a line dated outside every tour’s card dates or from before a card’s opening balance, a line for $0, and a line your bank later takes back. Greenroom used to throw nearly all of these away. It now keeps them, each with the reason it was left out and the same details as any other line (date, amount, name, account), so that every line your bank sends is accounted for. You can see them in the app under “Left out and set aside” and bring one into your pile. None of them is counted on a tour unless you do.
  • Charges you set aside: kept, as they always were, now with the reason picked (not a tour charge, already typed in, or a reimbursement), when, and who on the tour did it, so they can be listed and put back.
  • A tally of each read: every time Greenroom reads a bank it notes when, which bank, and how many lines the bank sent, the days they span, and where they went: already here, waiting, left out, and, as a number only, lines on accounts you switched off (by the account’s name) and money coming in. There are no merchant names and no amounts in it. It is how Greenroom checks that no line went missing. The newest 400 are kept, and full checks for 30 days.

The last three begin with the October 2026 update to the bank feed. Until that update is running for your banks, Greenroom still discards the lines it leaves out (all but the ones from before a card’s opening balance, which it keeps), keeps no reason or date with a charge that is set aside, and keeps no tally of its reads.

Everything is stored with Greenroom’s hosting provider, Supabase, which encrypts stored data.

Who can see it

  • The pile of charges waiting for a look on a tour: you, and that tour’s Tour Manager (a Manager on the tour whose tour role is Tour Manager), who can sort it. For an artist’s Off Tour book: you, and the Tour Manager of any of that artist’s tours. A line that is waiting for no tour in particular shows in every one of those piles, so each of those Tour Managers sees it too. That includes a line you bring in from “Left out and set aside” when it belongs to no tour. A line that was waiting on a tour you have since deleted shows in your own piles only, not in a Tour Manager’s.
  • The lines left out or set aside, while they stay there, and the tallies of each read: only you.
  • Charges on a tour: you, and the people you make Managers of that tour. People you invite as crew never receive the tour’s money at all: the database gives them a copy of the tour without it.
  • Charges on an artist’s Off Tour book: you, and the Managers of that artist’s tours.
  • Greenroom never sells your data or shares it with advertisers. It is used only to show your tours’ budgets, and it leaves Greenroom only in the one case described just below.

When you tap More info on a line you are sorting, that one line (its name, amount and date, and the name of its card or account without the digits), the names of the categories it could be filed under, and where the tour was in the days around it are sent to the reading service Greenroom uses, to work out what the line was. If you then tap Look it up online, that service searches the web for the name. For money coming in, the bank still gives Greenroom only the date and the amount, so what goes with those is what the app already says about that deposit and what the tour has logged for the nights around it. Nothing is sent until you tap, the answer is shown only to you, and nothing is filed, ticked or logged for you.

How long it’s kept

  • A bank’s Plaid key: until you disconnect that bank.
  • Your pile (the lines still waiting to be sorted): until you sort them, or until you disconnect your last bank, which clears them.
  • The record of what’s already been brought in (so nothing comes in twice), the lines you set aside and the lines left out: they stay after you disconnect, until you delete your account.
  • The tallies of each read: the newest 400, and full checks for 30 days. All of them go when you delete your account.
  • Charges on a tour: until you remove them. Deleting a tour hides it, with its charges, for everyone on it; it is kept so you can restore it, and is not erased. Deleting your whole account erases the tours you made and had deleted.

Deleting your data

  • Disconnect in Greenroom: Settings → Linked Cards → the trash next to a bank, or Disconnect every bank. Greenroom tells Plaid to close the connection and deletes its key right away. Disconnecting your last bank also clears the lines still waiting to be sorted. Lines already on a tour, lines you set aside and lines left out are kept. While no bank is linked the app does not show the list of what was left out and set aside; it shows again when you link a bank.
  • Or through Plaid: at my.plaid.com you can see and remove the apps connected to your banks. Greenroom stops being able to read anything right away.
  • Remove a charge, or a whole import, from a tour to erase it from that tour.
